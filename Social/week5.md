The rule of law \[This principle asserts that the law applies equally to all individuals, regardless of their status or position

explanation of all of the following elements\]

- equality before the law. **(из гпт)Equality before the law** means that every person is treated the same under the law, regardless of status, wealth, gender, race, religion, or position.

- Retroactivity. New law should not be used to punish someone for something they did before the law existed.

**Module 2: Defining Liberal Democracy through Indices**

Free, fair, and competitive elections \[define each word individually

- Free elections. Candidates are able to campaign and voters are able to vote, without coercion, repression or intimidation. No pressure whatsoever.
- Fair election. When the process is impartial, transparent, and conducted according to established laws and procedures.
- **(из гпт)Competitive elections** are elections in which two or more candidates or political parties genuinely compete for power.

\]

Reporters Without Borders' Press Freedom Index \[what is measures, criticisms of it

Assesses the state of media freedom in countries worldwide.  
The index evaluates factors such as:

- Media independence
- Legislative framework
- The safety of journalists

Criticism:

- RSF does not tell us how it calculated the final scores for each country, we can only guess at the reasons for this.
- Some scholars have also criticized the inclusion of 'economic pressures' as an indicators in RSF's Press Freedom Index. The indicator 'economic pressures' refers to issues such as journalists losing their jobs or media outlets closing down (can no longer make profit). As a result, countries where journalists are constitutionally protected and free to criticize the government, such as the United States, have recieved lower press freedom scores.

The lower rankings are partly due to:

- Widespread newsroom closures
- Job losses driven by economic factors

\]

Watchdog journalism \[The press acts as a watchdog, scrutinizing the actions of those in power and exposing any abuses, corruption, or malfeasance\]

The News of the World phone hacking scandal (2009-2011) \[basic details of the scandal. What the scandal shows us.

**Basic details: (из гпт)**  
Journalists at _News of the World_ illegally hacked voicemail messages of celebrities, politicians, and crime victims. Public outrage intensified when it was revealed that the phone of murdered teenager Millicent Dowler had been hacked. Police investigations followed, arrests were made, and the newspaper closed in 2011.

**What it shows: (из гпт)**

- Abuse of media power
- Lack of proper press regulation
- Importance of accountability and ethical journalism

**Podcast 6.1. Is liberal democracy in crisis? (из гпт)**

Reasons why some scholars say liberal democracy is in crisis \[explain the following\]:

**• Membership of political parties**  
Party membership has declined significantly since the 1970s. Fewer citizens engage directly in politics, creating a gap between political elites and ordinary voters.

**• Voter turnout**  
Turnout has fallen in many democracies, suggesting political disengagement and a belief that voting has limited impact.

**• Distrust**  
Public trust in politicians and political institutions has decreased, leading to concerns about a crisis of legitimacy.

Peter Mair \[name of his book, what he meant by 'the void'

**Book:** Ruling the Void: The Hollowing of Western Democracy

**"The void":** A growing gap between citizens and political elites. Democratic institutions remain, but real participation and accountability are weakened.

\]

Populist movements \[definition, examples of populist movements/politicians

**Definition:** Movements that claim to represent "the people" against a corrupt elite.

**Examples:** Donald Trump, Viktor Orbán, Five Star Movement.

Some argue populism threatens liberal democracy by weakening checks and minority rights; others see it as a response to public dissatisfaction.
