**5.2 Theoretical perspectives on regimes**

- **Teleological view of history** \[**Teleological view of history**:  
    The idea that history follows a purposeful, goal-directed path toward a final end or ultimate outcome.
- Events are seen not as random, but as moving toward a predetermined goal (e.g., progress, freedom, classless society, divine plan).

\]

- **Francis Fukuyama**:
  - Name of his book, year it was published. "The End of History and the Last Man". 1992
  - What he meant by the 'end of history'. He means that the liberal democracy is the final form of government for all nations and represents the pinnacle of human governance.
  - The global events that influenced his ideas. His view was profoundly influenced by the political climate of the late 20th century. The fall of the Berlin wall, the dissolution of the Soviet Union and the spread of democratic movements worldwide.
- **Steven Levitsky and Lucan Way**
  - their criticisms of Fukuyama. They argue that Fukuyama's teleological approach oversimplifies the complex nature of political development and fails to account for the resilience and adaptability of non-democratic regimes. Fukuyama views authoritarian regimes as incomplete of "transitional" forms of democracy.
  - what they mean by 'competitive authoritarianism'. They challenged the "transitional paradigm", which was dominant in the 1990s and early 2000s. "transitional paradigm" - once authoritarian regimes held elections or liberalized slightly, they were on an inevitable path towards democracy.

**5.3 Measuring political regimes**

**Democracy indices:**

- **Freedom House**
  - **names** of the 3 categories it uses to classify regimes. Free, partly free, not free. Countries are classified based on scores derived from a set of indicators:
    - electoral processes
    - political pluralism
    - freedom of expression
  - **criticisms** of Freedom House. The criticism can be divided into 3 categories:
    - lack of disaggregation. Only having 3 categories does not give us enough detail, and that these categories are too broad to be meaningful.
    - non-transparence and subjectivity. Freedom House provides very little info on its coding rules and criteria.
    - western political bias. Some scholars argue that Freedom House model implicitly assumes that liberal democracy is the only legitimate form of regime.
- **V-Dem**
  - Regimes of the World - **names** of the 4 regime categories, **definitions** of each of them, **examples** of countries for each regime type.

Instead of a single score, V-Dem provides separate indicators for various components of regimes such as:

- - 1. Media freedom
        - Judicial constraints
        - Civil liberties
        - Electoral fairness

4 regimes categories:

- - 1. Closed autocracy. Citizens do not have the right to choose their leaders through competitive elections. Examples: North Korea, China, Iran, Belarus.
        - Electoral autocracy. Multy-party elections take place, but election do not meet minimum democratic standards. Examples: Turkey, Russia, India.
        - Electoral democracy. The elections in which citizens choose their leades are regarded as free and fair. Lacks some liberal democracy's features:
            - the judicial system may not be fully independent from the government.
            - Some civil liberties may not be always fully guaranteed.

Examples: Brazil, Greece, Portugal, Poland

- - 1. Liberal democracy.
            - Holds free and fair elections
            - Upholds individual rights
            - Upholds the rule of law
            - Upholds the separation of powers

Governments actions are constrained by parliament and judiciary. Government cannot do what it wants.

Examples: France, Norway, Germany, Sweden

- **Polity**
  - Polity's **scoring system** for classifying regimes

It is designed to assess the level of democracy or autocracy in a country, based on observable features of political institutions.

The Polity dataset scores countries on a scale from -10 (full autocracy) to +10 (full democracy)

+6 to +10: democratic regime

\-6 to -10: autocratic regime

\-5 to 5: anocracies or hybrid regimes
