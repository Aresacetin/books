**4\. Domestic violence & child abuse.** ✅

**Concepts:** domestic violence, intimate partner violence (IPV), child abuse, patriarchal norms, stalking, gaslighting, coercive control, bride kidnapping, neglect

**Domestic violence:** is the pattern of behaviours used by one person to dominate or control another person with whom they have close family or friend relationship.

**Intimate partner violence (IPV):** is a specific form of domestic violence that occur between people who are or were in romantic or intimate relationship. IPV can be physical, phycological, sexual. Therefore, IPV is a pattern of behaviours by one person to abuse another person.

**Child abuse:** any behaviour by a caregiver, parent, or another adult that cause harm or threat health, survival, development, or dignity of the child.

**Patriarchal norms:** are social norms that men hold primary power and dominate in different parts of the society including politics, moral authorities, and property control.

**Stalking:** Repeated unwanted behaviours toward a specific person that would cause him/her feel fear, or significant emotional distress.

**Gaslighting:** is a form of psychological abuse in which the abuser makes the victim to doubt their own memory, perception, sanity or judgment and become relined on the abuser for what is real and truth.

**Coercive control:** is a form of physiological and emotional abuse that dominate, control, or manipulate a victim by bullying, isolation, and control over the victim's finance, social interactions, and employments.

**Bride kidnapping:** is practice in which women or girls are abducted to make them agree with the marriage regardless own their own will.

**Neglect:** is the condition that a parent or caregiver failed to provide the basic needs of a child which include education, food, and shelter.

**Theoretical approaches:** social learning theory (as an explanation for cycles of abuse, and approaches SLT suggests for breaking the cycle).

**According to social learning theory**: domestic violence and child abuse are learned behaviours and people who have had these experiences are likely to repeat the same behaviours in their own relationship.

The approaches that SLT suggest for breaking the cycle:

- Promoting positive behaviours
- Supporting individuals who have experienced the abuse.

**Empirical knowledge:**

- Different types of domestic violence
  - Physical abuse
  - Emotional abuse
  - Economical abuse
  - Phycological abuse
  - Sexual abuse
- Different types of child abuse (including neglect)
  - Physical abuse
  - Physiological abuse
  - Sexual abuse
  - Emotional harm
  - Neglect
- Factors preventing abuse victims seeking help
  - Fear of not being believed or lack of trust
  - Normalization of abusive behaviours
  - Not knowing how to report
  - Fear that the abuser may take revenge

**5\. Political regimes**

**Scholars:** Juan Jose Linz, Francis Fukuyama, Steven Levitsky & Lucan Way

**Concepts:** regime, democracy, autocracy, authoritarian regime, totalitarian regime, post-totalitarian

**Regime:** rules, norms, institutions that determined how government operates and how power is exercised.

**Democracy:** a system of government where the people hold the power to rule. this system has political equality, free and fair election, protection of fundamental human rights, rule of low

**Based on the form of government democracy**:

**Direct:** on every decision people vote and everyone has real decision-making power; this type works best on small group.

**Representative democracy:** people vote for leader who make decision and speak on behalf of the people; however, people are given the opportunity to vote on controversial or impartial topics.

**Democracy based on the quality of it:**

**Liberal democracy**: it has free election, rule of law, individual right, separation of power. In this form of democracy, the executives are mentored by parliament and judiciary.

**Electoral democracy:** citizens choose their leaders and are regarded as free and fair. It has real elections, freedom of parties; however, some civil rights are not always grantee always, judiciary is not fully independent.

**Autocracy:** a system of government by one person with absolute power.

**Closed autocracy:** is the system of government in a country that citizens do not have the right to choose their leader; either there are no election or signal party elections.

**Electrical autocracy:** countries that have election, but the election does not meet the minimal democratic standards because of falsifying votes.

**authoritarian regime:** a system that has concentrated and powerful power, but it is not absolute, power. It has a single leader or a small group of elites and exercises strong control over the state, but it is less intrusive than totalitarian regime.

Limited political pluralism, mentalities over ideology, limited mobilization.

**Totalitarian regime:** it is a more radical form of dictatorship that seeks to control both public and private life of citizens.

Official ideology, unlimited social control, mass mobilization, monopoly of power, interference into private life of people.

**Post-totalitarian regime:** it is a regime that has evolved from totalitarian regime but has not transitioned into democracy or stable authoritarianism.

**Theoretical approaches:** different systems for classifying regimes:

- democracy - non-democracy (autocracy)
- Linz: democracy - authoritarian - totalitarian (and post-totalitarian)
- Regimes of the World: (V-Dem) closed autocracy - electoral autocracy - electoral democracy, liberal democracy

**Empirical approaches:** two ways of measuring the level of democracy in the world: 1. counting the number of countries that are democracies; 2. by share of world population living in a democracy

**Empirical knowledge:** global and regional trends in regimes (is democracy increasing or decreasing?) according to V-Dem
